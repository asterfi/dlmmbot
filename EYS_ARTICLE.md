Token Selection:

I always watch the GMGN Trending section and set my filters before choosing a token. My rule is to look for tokens with 100k+ MCAP, at least 10 SOL in fees, 100K+ volume per minute, 0 dev fees, and of course, a good lore. If a token doesn’t meet these criteria, I simply won’t ape in. I always stick to my rules and never force a trade just because a token is pumping.
I’m also the type of guy who’s always watching the chart. I’m mainly looking for strong upward spikes because that’s one of my main entry criteria. If I see a token consistently making strong spikes and maintaining an upward trend, that’s usually when I’ll enter my position. I also pay close attention to the volume because I want to make sure there’s enough activity and momentum behind the move.


Entry Criteria:

My main entry criteria is volume. If the volume is over 100K per minute and the token has at least 10 SOL in fees, I’ll usually ape in immediately. If it doesn’t meet those criteria, I won’t enter the position.
KOL buys are another factor I look at. It’s not a requirement for me, but I consider it a bonus when deciding whether to enter.
I also pay close attention to fake volume. One way I check this is by looking at the fees. Right now, it’s easy for tokens to artificially boost their volume through bots or volume buying. For example, if a token has a 500K–1M MCAP but only has around 8–10 SOL in fees, that’s a red flag for me, and I won’t ape in.

Pump and Dump Strategies:

For pumping tokens, I always use Spot and mainly stick to three strategies: Spot SOL side(Default), tight range with 10–20 bins, and token-sided(risky).
Spot SOL side default:
I always enter first with Spot SOL-side using the default range. This is my safest entry and carries less risk compared to my other two strategies. Why? If the token keeps pumping after I enter SOL-side, I won’t gain much from the upside, but I’m also not taking the loss from the dump. downside risk. Most of the time, these coins will pump and then dump pretty fast, and that’s where I look to make my move and earn fees from it. Based on my observations over the past month, I’ve become more confident that the tokens I select won’t rug immediately. That’s why I’m comfortable starting with the Spot default strategy as my first entry.

Token Sided (Risky):
My criteria for entering a token-sided position is when the price breaks above the top of my default SOL-side range, the volume is above 300K per minute, and I see a strong spike on the chart. If the volume isn’t above 300K per minute, I won’t use the token-sided strategy. Instead, I’ll either use a different strategy or enter with a double-sided Spot position depending on the setup.

Why didn’t I remove my first position even though I wasn’t gaining much from it? I kept my first position as a backup plan in case my token-sided strategy went wrong or the token suddenly dumped hard. If the token dumps, my first position can catch the move and earn fees from it. And if the token rugs or enters a slow-moving phase, my first position can help cover some of the impermanent loss from my second, token-sided position. That’s the advantage for me because it can reduce my overall IL and potentially even help me stay profitable.



Tight Range (10-20 BINS):
My entry criteria for a tight-range position is 100K+ volume per minute. I usually use this strategy when I’ve been watching the token for 1–2 minutes and there hasn’t been any major dump, while the chart is still moving steadily within a small pump-and-dump range. If there’s no major dump and the volume is still holding above 100K per minute, I won’t hesitate to enter a tight-range position.

Dump token strategies:
Once I notice that the token has reached its peak volume or starts slowing down, I set up a wide-range Bid-Ask SOL-side position as a bonus play. I usually set it around the ATH or close to the ATH, with my range around -85% to -90% because I consider this a bonus stage rather than my main strategy. Why Bid-Ask? Because these are new and highly volatile tokens, so I want to play it safer. I don’t want to risk the profits I already made from my pump-token strategies. That’s why I use a wide-range Bid-Ask position and treat it as a bonus play on my end.

Risk Management
Since I started with only 1 SOL, my risk management was simple. I would only use 0.3 SOL for quick in-and-out plays. I never used my entire portfolio because I didn’t want to get wiped out from a single entry. At the same time, I was still figuring out which strategies fit my personality, so I kept my position size small. Eventually, I started making good profits from this approach and even grew that 0.3 SOL into 2 SOL through quick in-and-out plays.

For a token-sided position, I always use an amount of SOL that I’m comfortable losing completely. That’s the risk I’m willing to take when positioning on the token side. I don’t fully trust the token, and there’s always a fear that it could rug or dump hard. That’s why I keep my position size small—an amount that I’m okay with losing. Even if it goes to zero, it won’t affect my mindset or my overall portfolio.  For me, it’s all about managing the risk before entering the position.
